## 1. Text on the image: the 20% rule, what Meta says, safe zones (4:5, 9:16)

### Takeaway
The 20% rule has not been enforced since 2020/2021 (old advice). Meta's position, as secondary sources report it, is still that **less text on the image gets more reach at lower cost**. The working rule is to keep the image under about 20% text (some say 10–15%) and put everything else in the primary text and headline. For layout, use 4:5 (1080×1350) as the main format. For 9:16, keep about 14% at the top, about 20–35% at the bottom and about 6% at each side free of text.

### Cited Findings
- Meta retired the 20% rule as a rejection reason. Sources disagree on the date (Sept. 2020 vs. 2021). Ads are no longer rejected for too much text — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/facebook-removes-the-20-text-limit-on-ad-images/381844/); [Social News Desk](https://www.socialnewsdesk.com/blog/facebooks-20-rule-is-no-more/); [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/facebook-text-overlay)
- Meta still says ads with less image text perform better. Text-heavy images "may see reduced delivery and higher costs" (Meta position reported secondhand) — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/facebook-text-overlay); [Instapage](https://instapage.com/blog/facebook-20-text-rule); [Jon Loomer Pubcast](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/videos/text-in-images-for-meta-ads/)
- Target of 10–15% text area, preview each placement. This is a vendor recommendation, not a Meta figure. Logos with text count toward the total — [Adamigo](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-20-text-rule-what-you-need-to-know); [Orbee KB](https://kb.orbee.com/meta-20-rule)
- Most of the text belongs in the primary text, headline and description fields, not on the image — [Straight North](https://www.straightnorth.com/blog/meta-ads-creative-best-practices-that-convert/); [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/image-optimization-for-meta-ads)
- Meta recommends 1:1 or 4:5 in the feed. 4:5 (1080×1350) gives more vertical space on mobile. Start from a 4:5 master and customize per placement — [Jon Loomer, aspect ratios](https://www.jonloomer.com/recommended-aspect-ratios-by-placement-for-meta-ad-creative/); [Adobe GenStudio Meta template](https://experienceleague.adobe.com/en/docs/genstudio-for-performance-marketing/user-guide/templates/channel-best-practice/meta-template); [Buffer Ad Specs 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/facebook-ad-specs)
- "4:5 beats 1:1 by up to 15% in the feed" comes from Billo's own tests (vendor, directional only) — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-best-practices)
- Over 98% of Meta users are on mobile, so design for small screens and fast scrolling — [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-ad-creative-best-practices)
- **Safe zones 9:16 (2026):** Several vendor blogs say Meta merged the Stories and Reels safe zones in **March 2026**, expressed as percentages: **top 14%, bottom 20–35%, sides 6% each**. Adnova uses a 35% bottom. Billo converts this to 1440×2560: about 358 px top, about 512–896 px bottom, about 87 px per side — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones); [Adnova](https://adnova.ai/blogs/meta-ad-safe-zones-guide)
- Older pixel figures (1080×1920): keep about 270 px at the top and about 672 px at the bottom free, plus about 65 px per side. Stories bottom 20% (380 px), Reels 35% (670 px) because of interactive elements — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-reels-safe-zone/); [Adsuploader](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)
- Narrower variant for boosted Reels: 1010×1280 px safe area, 220 px from the top and 420 px from the bottom — [Ignite Social Media](https://www.ignitesocialmedia.com/content-creation/what-are-the-safe-zones-for-tiktoks-and-instagram-reels/)

### Inferences
- For our layout (photo + kicker + headline + subline + CTA), all text should sit together in **one text block**, take up at most about 20% of the image area, and stay inside the strictest safe zone. On 9:16 that means between about 14% from the top and about 35% from the bottom, with about 6% margin at the sides.
- A single 4:5 master with the text block in the upper or middle third can be extended to 9:16 by adding photo area above and below without moving the text.
- The CTA on the image is optional, because Meta shows its own CTA button ("Termin buchen"). If space is tight, cut the on-image CTA first, before the headline.

### Gaps
- Meta's original wording (Business Help Center, Ads Guide) could not be opened (fetch blocked). The March 2026 safe zone update is documented only through vendor blogs, which also disagree on the bottom margin (20% vs. 35%).
- No 2025/26 study quantifies how much reach or cost text-heavy images lose today.

## 2. Headline on the image: word count, benefit- or offer-first, price badges, before/after vs. result, faces, UGC vs. polished

### Takeaway
There is no Meta figure for the number of words on the image. Practitioners agree on the principle: **the offer must be understood in under 2–3 seconds**, with one message and one CTA per ad. Practical limit: headline about 3–7 words. Meta's ad headline field (below the image) is cut off at about 27–40 characters, so it has to work on its own too. Real studio photos beat stock photos. Lo-fi/UGC no longer stands out (42% of top ads); on Meta, "polished UGC" is recommended.

### Cited Findings
- Analysis of salon chain ads (Great Clips, Drybar, Ulta): a viewer should grasp the offer **in under two seconds**, with **one** clear CTA — [GetHookd, 5 best salon Facebook ads](https://www.gethookd.ai/learn/5-best-salon-facebook-ads-examples/)
- Esthetician guide: the offer should be understandable within about **three seconds**. For brows and lashes, use a "transformation offer" with a strong visual. Generic stock images usually perform worse than real photos from the studio — [Graphed, Facebook Ads for Estheticians](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-estheticians)
- Lash guide: creative is the biggest lever. Show style comparisons, process and hygiene. Offers should be **specific, bundled packages built around an outcome** (first-time set, natural set, consultation) instead of blanket discounts. Deep discounts attract bargain hunters who don't come back for refills. Judge by cost per booked appointment, not by clicks — [Graphed, Facebook Ads for Lash Artists](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-lash-artists)
- Example offers from salon ads: "$49 signature facial for first-time clients", "free consultation + $20 off first service" (US examples) — [Graphed, Beauty Salons](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-beauty-salons); [GoHighLevel Local Ads Playbook](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000005250-boosts-over-balayage-the-local-ads-playbook-for-beauty-salons)
- Headline field below the image: vendors say Meta recommends **about 27 characters**, and longer text is cut off on mobile. Another guide says 40 characters. Primary text should stay at **≤125 characters**, with the hook and offer in the first 125 characters. In a sample of long-running ads the median was about 198 characters — [Cropink, 2026](https://cropink.com/facebook-ad-copy-examples); [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/facebook-ad-copy); [Instapage](https://instapage.com/blog/meta-image-ad-specs)
- Recommended headline test order: (1) offer headline ("what they get"), (2) specificity headline (number/detail), (3) question headline that answers an objection — [AdMake](https://admakeai.com/blog/facebook-ad-copy-examples)
- Put the offer in the headline ("Save 40%" instead of a generic product description) — [Moda, static ads](https://moda.app/blog/static-ads)
- Meta's "first 3 seconds" rule officially applies to **video**. For static ads, the image carries the hook — [Madgicx](https://madgicx.com/blog/meta-ad-creative-best-practices); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/marketing/social-media/effective-facebook-ads)
- One message per ad, a clear idea immediately, a format that fits the placement — [Billo, Best Practices 2026](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-best-practices); [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/meta-creative-best-practices/)
- Image first: people look at the image before the text (DE) — [Neil Patel DE](https://neilpatel.com/de/blog/facebook-werbeanzeigen-verbessern/)
- **UGC vs. polished:** Motion (analysis of over $100M in ad spend, 2025): **42% of top-spending ads are lo-fi**, so lo-fi no longer stands out as different. For Meta, Motion recommends "more polished UGC" (for TikTok, native styles) — [Motion Creative Trends](https://motionapp.com/creative-trends); [Motion, Lo-fi UGC 2025](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/best-way-to-make-lo-fi-ugc-ads-in-2025-3-pro-examples/)
- AppsFlyer (Q1 2025–Q2 2026): neither format wins as a category. Attributes that work in video often work the other way in static ads — [AppsFlyer Creative Optimization 2026](https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/guides/creative-optimization/)
- Share of static ads stays stable: 2024 41.5%, 2025 42.2% — [The Brief](https://www.thebrief.ai/blog/state-of-ad-creation/)

### Inferences
- **Rule for our layout:** kicker 1–3 words (who/what: "Neukundinnen", "Wimpern-Lifting"), headline **max. 5–7 words**, which states the benefit or offer as a noun ("Wimpern, die 4 Wochen halten"). Subline **max. 8–10 words** (one fact: duration, price or location). CTA 2–3 words. In total **about 15–20 words on the image at most**, ideally fewer. Today's complaint ("far too much text") points to well above that.
- **Hierarchy:** the headline takes most of the size (roughly 2–3× the subline). Show the price or offer as a separate badge or number in the image when there is one ("79 €"), because numbers are grasped faster than sentences (no study found for this, practitioner consensus, see Gaps).
- **Photo:** a real **result photo** (close-up of lashes, brows or nails on a real face) in the studio's own light. Avoid stock photos. A face or eye area as the subject fits "transformation" (Graphed). Polished but real ("polished UGC") rather than an advertising studio look.
- Primary text and the headline field carry the details (what's included, how long, where, booking). The image carries only the offer and the benefit.

### Gaps
- No hard data (A/B tests) on the best word count on the image, on price badges vs. no badge, or on faces vs. no faces for beauty statics. The "faces in the image" recommendation has no 2025/26 source for static ads.
- No conversion data comparing before/after shots and result shots for lashes, brows or nails (the sources only say "transformation visual").
- Practically no German beauty sources. Most are US vendor or software blogs (Graphed, GoHighLevel, Booksy).

## 3. Hook formulas for beauty and proven headline templates for local service businesses

### Takeaway
The recurring beauty angles are **"Wake up ready"** (waking up already done, saving time in the morning), a natural look, getting past objections (pain, trust, qualification), and an outcome-based first-time package. Few templates have data behind them. They are mostly collections of examples.

### Cited Findings
- PMU brow/lip ad: waking up with fuller brows and more defined lips that still look natural. Lash ad: an effortless look right after waking up. Both come from unattributed copy collections, so treat them as templates and not as proven winners — [Google Docs copy collection](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1_tgYh0s5v-MTbuudWIuXestC9S9DVOlCcCJ5CPG2aT8/export?format=pdf); [Google Docs copy collection 2](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1IBwGsQFQm_V-tD3XXtVi5Ia43uxbNZfKpv-q1QOhbmM/export?format=pdf)
- PMU objections to answer in the ad: "Does it hurt?", "Can I trust the artist?", "What qualifications?". Answer with certificates and review scores — [same source](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1_tgYh0s5v-MTbuudWIuXestC9S9DVOlCcCJ5CPG2aT8/export?format=pdf)
- Lash ad formats: a question about self-care leading into the "wake up" promise. Pain-point templates like "Stop [pain point]" — [StyleSeat, lash advertising](https://www.styleseat.com/blog/6-successful-advertising-ideas-for-lash-extension-business/); [Clicksgeek, lash studios](https://clicksgeek.com/facebook-ads-for-lash-studios/)
- Lash ads should lead to a **lash-specific** page (benefit, result photos, price, reviews, location) — [Graphed, Lash Artists](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-lash-artists)
- If an ad gets no attention, the hook or the visual is usually to blame — [Graphed, Lash Artists](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-lash-artists)
- Motion collects "the best-performing 24 ad hooks for Meta 2025" (video talk, content not fetchable) — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/the-best-performing-24-ad-hooks-for-meta-in-2025/)

### Inferences (template suggestions derived from the angles above, not tested)
- **Outcome + duration:** "Wimpern, die 4 Wochen halten" · "Brauen, die morgens schon sitzen"
- **Time saved / waking up ready:** "Aufwachen und fertig sein" · "Morgens 15 Minuten länger schlafen"
- **Offer first:** "Neukundinnen: Wimpernverlängerung 79 €" · "Gel-Nägel, 3 Wochen haltbar – 45 €"
- **Occasion:** "Hochzeit im Frühling? Brauen jetzt planen" · "Urlaubsfertig: Wimpern ohne Mascara"
- **Answering an objection:** "Natürlich statt angeklebt" · "Laser – zertifiziert, ohne Rasieren" (pay attention to the treatment-specific advertising rules from the other research; see the project's own hard rules in CLAUDE.md, e.g. "dauerhaft" only with a note on the number of sessions)
- Each of these angles is a **separate concept** in the Andromeda sense (see section 4), not just a variation.

## 4. Meta Andromeda and Advantage+ (2024–2026): creative diversity, number of ads, concepts, primary text vs. image text

### Takeaway
Andromeda is Meta's AI retrieval stage (officially introduced on the Meta Engineering blog on Dec. 2, 2024, with a follow-up on Oct. 28, 2026). It picks a few thousand candidates out of tens of millions of ads. In practice: **the creative is now the targeting.** What matters is **genuinely different concepts** (angle, hook, look, format). Near-identical variations are grouped together and get little budget. Meta no longer sets a fixed number of ads. For small budgets, practitioners recommend about 3–8 genuinely different creatives per ad set.

### Cited Findings
- Meta Engineering blog, Dec. 2, 2024: Andromeda is the **retrieval stage** of the multi-stage ad system. It selects "from tens of millions of ad candidates into a few thousand relevant ad candidates" — [Engineering at Meta, 2024](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval/)
- Follow-up, Oct. 28, 2026: next-generation retrieval scores every eligible ad at close to ranking quality, at "over one billion ads per second" — [Engineering at Meta, 2026](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/10/28/ml-applications/meta-andromeda-next-gen-ads-retrieval-gpu-co-design/)
- PPC Land reports that Meta says Andromeda has raised conversions on Facebook since 2025 (headline "lifted Facebook conversions 2-4%") — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-andromeda-lifted-facebook-conversions-2-4-since-2025/)
- In early 2025 Meta dropped its old recommendation of at most 6 ads per ad set. Volume only helps if the budget supports learning — [Jon Loomer, Creative Diversification](https://www.jonloomer.com/meta-andromeda-creative-diversification/); [Common Thread Co.](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/meta-andromeda-roas-creative-strategy-2026)
- "Creative diversification is the new targeting" (Jon Loomer Pubcast, Sept. 3, 2025) — [Jon Loomer Pubcast](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/creative-diversification-is-the-new-targeting/)
- 30 variations of the same concept bring no gain and can split the budget. To the algorithm, 20 ads can be "slight edits of the same creative" — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-creative-diversity-485751); [Convert](https://www.convert.com/blog/growth-marketing/meta-andromeda-cro-guide/)
- Numbers vary: minimum 6–8 per ad set and 15–25 optimal from $5K/month; for **small businesses 3–5 genuinely different creatives are enough**; by budget: 8–12 per month under $10K — [Superads](https://superads.ai/blog/creative-diversity-in-ads); [Excite Media](https://www.excitemedia.com.au/meta-creative-diversification/); [Hunch](https://www.hunchads.com/blog/meta-andromeda-explained)
- Jon Loomer tested 25 and 50 ads per ad set and saw no clear advantage — [Jon Loomer, How many ads is too many](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/how-many-ads-is-too-many/)
- The often-quoted Meta stat "1 ad set with 25 diverse creatives beats 5×5: +17% conversions, −16% cost" has **no primary Meta source** — [Search summary of several blogs, e.g. Chatterbuzz](https://www.chatterbuzzmedia.com/blog/meta-andromeda-creative-targeting/)
- "Entity ID" is practitioner shorthand for Andromeda's grouping of near-identical ads, not an official Meta term. In 2026 a "Creative Similarity Score" is said to appear in Ads Manager. The claim that a score above 60% triggers suppression is **unconfirmed** (one fact check rates it "half true", Meta publishes no thresholds) — [Atria](https://www.tryatria.com/blog/andromeda-meta-ads); [nikhil.pro](https://nikhil.pro/meta-ads-creative-similarity-explained/); [Clutch](https://clutch.co/resources/meta-andromeda-update-2026)
- Motion talk "Andromeda 2.0 explained" (July 2026), not fetchable — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/andromeda-2-0-explained-how-meta-s-algorithm-actually-picks-your-winning-ads/)

### Inferences
- For one studio on a small budget: **3–5 genuinely different concepts** per ad set (e.g. offer/price · waking up ready · occasion · objection/trust · social proof). Each concept gets its own photo subject (not the same photo with different text). The same layout grid can stay (it is a brand frame), but **photo, angle and headline** have to differ.
- Andromeda presumably also reads text and image together. Because image text acts as a "concept signal", a clear, short on-image headline tells the system what the ad is about, and the primary text adds detail. (Plausible conclusion, not confirmed by Meta.)
- Swapping only the colour, font or a word in the headline is a "variation", not a new concept. It counts as roughly one ad.

### Gaps
- Meta's own wording on creative diversification (Business Help Center / Advantage+ guide) could not be read directly.
- How Andromeda weights on-image text compared with the primary text is not documented.
- Advantage+ "creative enhancements" (automatic text overlays and cropping) could not be researched further due to the fetch block. Recommendation for the report: check which automatic enhancements the template turns on, because they can place additional text over the image.

## 5. Benchmarks (CTR, CPL) for beauty lead and booking ads

### Takeaway
There are only US industry averages. LocaliQ 2025, beauty lead campaigns: **CTR 2.55%, CPC $3.06, CPL $51.42, CVR 5.29%**. Traffic campaigns: CTR about 1.8–2.7%, CPC about $0.50–0.74. No reliable beauty CPL figures were found for DACH. German CPC estimates are €0.30–2.50 and CPM €5–15.

### Cited Findings
- LocaliQ 2025, Beauty & Personal Care: traffic CPC $0.74 / CTR 1.81%. **Leads: CPC $3.06, CPL $51.42, CTR 2.55%, CVR 5.29%** — [LocaliQ Facebook Benchmarks](https://localiq.com/blog/facebook-advertising-benchmarks/)
- WordStream 2025: average CPL across all industries $27.66 (+20.94% compared with the year before). Beauty traffic CPC −21% compared with 2024 — [WordStream 2025](https://wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025)
- WordStream 2026: Beauty & Personal Care traffic CTR 2.73%, CPC $0.50. Search Engine Land names Beauty at **$50.91 CPL** among the most expensive lead industries. Average CPL across all industries in 2026: $27.39 — [WordStream 2026](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026); [WordStream PDF 2026](https://www.wordstream.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ws-guide-facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026.pdf); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/facebook-ad-costs-fall-as-traffic-and-lead-performance-improves-report-488433)
- Older WordStream figures (year unclear, probably before 2025): Beauty CPC $0.94, CTR 1.51% (old, do not use as current) — [WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks)
- Germany 2026 (agency estimates): CPC €0.40–2.00, CPM €5–15. Others say €0.30–2.50 CPC — [Influee DE](https://influee.co/de/blog/how-much-do-facebook-ads-cost); [ostend.digital](https://ostend.digital/meta-ads-kosten-kompass/)
- Measure cost per booked appointment, not per click or lead — [Graphed, Lash Artists](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-lash-artists)
- Salon test guidance: $10–20 per day, 3–5 day test window (US vendor) — [Graphed, Beauty Salons](https://graphed.com/blog/facebook-ads-for-beauty-salons)

### Inferences
- As a rough planning anchor for DACH: link CTR about 1.5–2.5% as "normal", above 2.5% as good. CPL for beauty services is probably clearly below the US figure of about $50 for low-priced services (lashes, brows, nails) and closer to it for laser or PMU. This is not backed by data and has to be checked against the first studio's own numbers.
- A short, offer-first image is mainly expected to raise **CTR and click quality**. Whether CPL falls depends on how well the landing page and booking continue the same offer (same wording, same price).

### Gaps
- No German or European beauty-specific CTR/CPL/cost-per-booking data found (only US averages and general German CPC ranges).
- No case studies with before/after numbers from "text-heavy → text-light image" for salons.
